# Getting North Point Creative Found Online

A working playbook for Google, Yell and the rest. Written for the site as it
stands on 15 August 2026.

Two halves to this, and only one of them is code:

- **On the site** — done, in this branch. Details in [What changed in the code](#what-changed-in-the-code) at the bottom.
- **Off the site** — the listings, the profiles and the reviews. Nobody can do
  this part from a code editor: every one of them needs your email, your phone
  and, in Google's case, a video of you. That is what the rest of this document
  is for.

Work top to bottom. It is ordered by what actually brings enquiries, not by
what is quickest.

---

## 0. First, the one rule everything else depends on

Search engines decide that a listing on Yell, a profile on Google and this
website are all the *same business* by matching the name, address and phone
number — the "NAP". Character for character. `Suite 2, 14 Bold St` and
`14 Bold Street, Suite 2` read as two different businesses to a machine, and
two half-trusted businesses rank worse than one fully-trusted one.

So decide these **once**, write them down, and paste the identical text into
every single listing below.

```
Business name:  North Point Creative
Locality:       Liverpool, Merseyside
Country:        United Kingdom
Phone:          07345 672773
WhatsApp:       https://wa.me/447345672773
Email:          hello@northpointcreative.co.uk
Website:        https://northpointcreative.co.uk
Hours:          Monday to Friday, 9am–5pm (calls and messages)
                Email answered seven days a week
Category:       Website designer
Description:    North Point Creative is a boutique web design studio in
                Liverpool, building custom, hand-designed websites for
                businesses across the UK. Fixed prices from £295, quoted
                within 24 hours, and every site is yours to own outright.
```

### The phone number: one format, everywhere

`07345 672773` is now on the site — in the structured data, the Contact
section and the footer — and in the international form `+44 7345 672773` where
machines read it.

The rule from here is boring and it matters: **type it the same way every
time.** `07345 672773`, with that single space. Not `07345672773`, not
`+447345 672773`. Google matches businesses across the web on an exact
name/address/phone string, so two formats across twenty listings reads as two
half-corroborated businesses instead of one solid one.

Two things worth knowing, neither urgent:

- **A mobile is perfectly acceptable.** Google accepts mobile numbers for
  service-area businesses and thousands of Liverpool sole traders list one.
  It is a marginally weaker local signal than an `0151` landline, which is
  read as tied to the city — if you ever want to close that gap, a `0151` VoIP
  line runs about £5–10/mo (bOnline, Vonage, 8x8) and can forward to the same
  handset. Not worth doing now; worth knowing later.
- **This number is about to be public.** Expect cold sales calls, particularly
  in the fortnight after the directory listings go live. That is the cost of
  being findable, and it is worth paying — just do not be alarmed by it.

### Get the WhatsApp Business app

Your hours are 9–5 weekdays, but WhatsApp messages will arrive at 9pm on a
Sunday. The free [WhatsApp Business](https://business.whatsapp.com) app
(replaces the normal app on the same number) solves this properly:

- **Away messages** that auto-reply outside your hours, so a Sunday enquiry
  gets an immediate "thanks, I'll come back to you Monday morning" instead of
  silence
- **A greeting message** for first-time enquiries
- **A business profile** showing your hours, address area, email and website
  inside the chat itself — another consistent citation, and one prospects
  actually look at
- **Labels** to separate live enquiries from clients from suppliers
- **Quick replies** — saved answers for "what does a website cost?", which is
  the message you will get most

Set the hours in the app to match everywhere else: Mon–Fri, 9am–5pm.

### And decide the address question

You are a home-based or by-appointment studio. Google supports this properly:
it is called a **service-area business**. You give Google your address so it
can verify you exist, then tick the box to **hide the address publicly** and
list service areas instead. Everyone else in your position does this. Do not
invent an address, and do not use a virtual office you do not actually sit in
— Google suspends profiles for both, and getting reinstated takes weeks.

## 1. Google Business Profile — do this first, today

This is not one item on a list of thirty. For a local service business, Google
Business Profile is somewhere near half of the whole job. It is what puts you
in the map pack — the three businesses with the map that appear above the
normal blue links for a search like "web designer Liverpool". Those three
positions take the overwhelming majority of local clicks.

**Set it up at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com).**

1. **Name:** `North Point Creative`. Exactly that — no "Web Design Liverpool"
   bolted on the end. Keyword-stuffed names get reported and suspended, and
   competitors do report them.
2. **Primary category:** `Website designer`. This one field carries more
   ranking weight than anything else on the profile — it decides which
   searches you are even eligible for.
3. **Secondary categories:** `Web hosting company`, `Graphic designer`,
   `Marketing agency` — only ones you genuinely do.
4. **Address:** enter it, then tick *"I deliver goods and services to my
   customers"* and hide the address.
5. **Service areas:** Liverpool, Wirral, St Helens, Warrington, Southport,
   Chester, Manchester. (Google caps this at 20; do not list the whole UK —
   an over-broad service area dilutes you.)
6. **Verification:** you will almost certainly get **video verification**. You
   record one unbroken clip showing your workspace, your equipment, and
   something tying you to the business — a bank statement, an invoice, branded
   material. Takes five minutes; approval takes up to five working days. Have
   something branded to hand before you start recording.
7. **Hours:** `Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00`. Same as the site, same as the
   WhatsApp Business app. Do not leave this blank — a profile with no hours
   gets shown less in "open now" searches, which is a large share of mobile
   local searches.
8. **Services:** add each package — Basic Website £295, Standard £495,
   Premium £750, Care Plan £35/mo — with the descriptions from your price
   list. These show directly in the profile and are read as ranking signals.
9. **Photos:** at least ten. Your compass logo, the portrait, and screenshots
   of all four client sites. Profiles with photos get materially more clicks
   than profiles without.
10. **Products:** add the four packages here too. Yes, it duplicates Services.
    Both surfaces display separately in search.

Then **keep it alive**. A profile posted to monthly outranks an identical
profile left untouched. One Google Post a month — a new project going live, a
note on turnaround times — is enough.

### Reviews: the second-biggest lever, and it is free

Number of reviews and recency of reviews are among the strongest local ranking
factors there are. You already have four happy clients quoted on your own site.
Those quotes are worth far more sitting on your Google profile.

Google Business Profile gives you a short review link (Profile → Ask for
reviews). Send it to Ryan, Andrew, Lee and Holly this week, individually, with
a sentence that reminds them what you built. Four genuine reviews will put you
ahead of most one-person studios in Liverpool.

Then make it routine: request a review at handover on every project, while
they are still pleased with the work.

**Do not**: buy reviews, review yourself, or add review markup to your own site
for reviews you collected yourself. Google stopped showing star ratings for
self-collected reviews on your own site in 2019, and both of the first two are
grounds for profile suspension. (This is why I deliberately did *not* add
`aggregateRating` to the site's structured data, even though the testimonials
are real — it would do nothing for you and carries risk.)

## 2. Tell the search engines the site exists

Free, fifteen minutes, and without them you are guessing about everything else.

| Tool | Link | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Search Console** | [search.google.com/search-console](https://search.google.com/search-console) | Add the domain, verify (DNS TXT record is the durable method), submit `https://northpointcreative.co.uk/sitemap.xml`, then hit *Request Indexing* on the homepage |
| **Bing Webmaster Tools** | [bing.com/webmasters](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) | Import straight from Search Console in one click. Worth doing — Bing feeds ChatGPT's web results |

Search Console is also the only honest scoreboard you will get: it shows the
actual searches people used to find you and where you ranked. Check it monthly.

If you would rather verify by meta tag than DNS, there is a marked slot for the
tags near the top of `index.html`.

## 4. UK directories — including Yell

Two things to understand before you start, because they will save you money.

**First: what these are actually for.** A directory listing rarely brings
enquiries directly. What it does is create a *citation* — another place on the
web stating that North Point Creative, Liverpool, `0151 XXX XXXX` exists. Google
counts consistent citations as corroboration that you are a real business, and
that feeds your map pack ranking. Volume matters less than consistency, which
is why section 0 comes first.

**Second: expect the sales calls.** Yell in particular will phone you hard
about paid packages once you claim a free listing. The free listing is the part
worth having. Paid directory advertising is, for a studio of your size, almost
always worse value than the same money spent on Google Ads or on nothing at all.

Local relevance is exactly what you are trying to build, so these punch above

- **Liverpool BID Company** — [liverpoolbidcompany.com](https://www.liverpoolbidcompany.com)
- **Marketing Liverpool** and local business networking groups (BNI, 4Networking Liverpool)

Do not buy a "we'll submit your site to 500 directories" package. Those build
hundreds of junk citations with slightly-wrong details, which is precisely the
inconsistency that damages the trust you are trying to establish. Fifteen
correct listings beat five hundred sloppy ones, every time.

## 5. Social profiles

Not for the social media — for the fact that these profiles rank on page one
for `"North Point Creative"`, which is what a prospect searches after you have
pitched them. An empty page-one result is a bad first impression.

- **Facebook Page** — required for a Facebook/Instagram business presence, and Facebook Pages rank strongly for brand searches
- **Instagram** — the obvious one for a design studio. Post the work
- **LinkedIn Company Page** — where B2B clients check you are real
- **Behance or Dribbble** — portfolio surfaces with their own search traffic

Once each exists, add its URL to the `sameAs` array in the site's structured
data (marked with a comment in `index.html`). That array is the line that tells
Google "all of these profiles are this one business". It ties the whole thing
together and it is currently empty — **it is now the only thing still missing
from the code.**

While you are setting these up: put `wa.me/447345672773` in every bio field
that allows a link, and the same `07345 672773` in every phone field. Instagram
and Facebook both let you add a WhatsApp button to the profile directly.

## 6. Links — where the real ranking gains are

Citations get you trusted. Links get you ranked. You are sitting on the easiest
link opportunity a web designer has:

### Ask your four clients for a footer credit

Dr.Shrooms, Knife Crime Matters, Beyond The Gate and Early Recovery Coaching
all have sites you built. A line in each footer —

```html
<a href="https://northpointcreative.co.uk">Site by North Point Creative</a>
```

— is four relevant, contextual backlinks from four real, live UK websites.
That is worth more than every directory in section 4 put together. Most clients
say yes if you ask at handover; ask the existing four retrospectively. Make it
part of your standard handover from now on.

One caution: keep the anchor text natural ("Site by North Point Creative"),
identical across all of them, and never `web design Liverpool`. Exact-match
keyword anchors repeated across sites you control is a recognised spam pattern.

### Then, in rough order of value

- **Case studies on your own site.** See section 7 — this is the big structural one
- **Beyond The Gate and Knife Crime Matters are newsworthy.** Prison
  rehabilitation and knife crime campaigns are exactly what the *Liverpool Echo*
  and local BBC cover. A local news piece mentioning who built the site is a
  high-authority local link you cannot buy
- **Liverpool business blogs and podcasts** — one-person studios with a clear
  point of view (yours: honesty about AI) are easy guests
- **The AI transparency angle is genuinely distinctive.** "Human made, AI built"
  is a real position most studios are hiding from. That is a pitch to a design
  publication, not just a section on your homepage

## 7. The structural limit of a one-page site

Everything above works with the site exactly as it is. But there is a ceiling
built into the current design and you should know where it is.

**A single page can realistically rank for one cluster of searches.** Yours is
`web design Liverpool` and close variants. It cannot also rank for
`web design Wirral`, `ecommerce website design Liverpool`,
`charity website design`, or `how much does a website cost UK` — because
Google ranks *pages*, not sites, and you have one page.

When you are ready to grow beyond the map pack, the highest-value additions, in
order:

1. **Four case study pages** — one per project (`/work/beyond-the-gate`, etc.).
   You already have the images, the results and the client quotes. These rank
   for the client's sector, they prove capability far better than a thumbnail,
   and they give you somewhere to send prospects
2. **Service-area pages** — `/web-design-wirral`, `/web-design-warrington`,
   `/web-design-chester`. These must have genuinely different content on them,
   not the same page with the town swapped. A near-duplicate set is worse than
   nothing
3. **A handful of articles answering what your buyers actually type** —
   "what does a small business website cost in 2026", "do I own my website if I
   use Wix", "should I let AI build my website". You are already writing this
   material as FAQ answers. Full pages of it earn links and rank for hundreds
   of long-tail searches you will never get otherwise

Also worth knowing: **the intro loader is a conversion cost.** The content is
in the HTML so it will be indexed properly, but a full-screen gate the visitor
has to click through before seeing anything delays your Largest Contentful
Paint and gives every impatient visitor an exit. It is a deliberate design
choice and a nice one — just be aware you are paying for it. If enquiries are
lower than the traffic suggests they should be, that is the first thing I would
test removing.

ranking for `web design Liverpool` is a slower game — that is months, and it is
mostly won by the links in section 6, not by anything on the page.

## What changed in the code

All committed on this branch.

**`index.html`**

- Replaced the repo's old, unrelated "Dan's Brand Design Studio" page with the
  current North Point Creative site. The old version is still in git history at
  commit `dfc210a` if it is ever needed
- **Title** is now `Web Design Liverpool | North Point Creative`. The search
  term leads instead of the brand name, because nobody who does not already
  know you searches for "North Point Creative"
- **Meta description** rewritten to lead on the location and the service
- **Structured data** rebuilt as a linked `@graph`: `WebSite`, `WebPage`,
  `Person` (you) and the `ProfessionalService` studio node, all cross-referenced
  by `@id` so Google reads them as one connected entity rather than four loose
  objects. Added `geo` coordinates, `addressRegion`, an expanded `areaServed`
  covering the seven towns, `logo` as a proper `ImageObject`, `foundingDate`,
  `currenciesAccepted`, a `contactPoint`, and `url` + `availability` on every
  package offer
- **Phone, hours and WhatsApp added** — `telephone` in international form,
  `openingHoursSpecification` for Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00, and three separate
  `contactPoint` entries: the phone with its weekday hours, WhatsApp with its
  `wa.me` link, and email marked as available all week
- **One slot still deliberately empty**: `sameAs`, marked with a comment. It
  needs real profile URLs and wrong data there is worse than absent data
- **`geo.region` / `geo.placename` / `ICBM` meta tags** added — legacy, but
  still read by several of the directories in section 4
- **A marked slot** for the Google and Bing verification meta tags
- **Footer** now carries a machine-readable NAP block — name, locality, phone,
  email, hours — plus a service-areas line, so the towns you cover appear as
  crawlable text and not only inside the JSON
- **Contact section** now lists Telephone and WhatsApp alongside Email, each
  with its own hours underneath rather than in a separate row
- **A WhatsApp button**, fixed bottom-right on every screen. Built in the
  studio's own materials — a noir plate with a brass hairline, the glyph left
  in WhatsApp's green so it is recognised instantly — rather than the usual
  green bubble, which would have fought the page. It collapses to a circle on
  phones, stays hidden until the loader lifts, and stands itself down over the
  contact section, where the form is already asking for the same thing and
  where on a phone it would sit on top of the submit button. Opens a chat with
  a prefilled first message
- The existing FAQ structured data was already correct and is untouched

**New files** — these three go in the **root of the web host**, not just the
repo, or they do nothing:

- **`robots.txt`** — opens the site to all crawlers and points to the sitemap.
  AI/assistant crawlers left deliberately allowed
- **`sitemap.xml`** — the homepage plus image entries for the five key images.
  Submit this URL in Search Console
- **`site.webmanifest`** — the page was already asking for this file and getting
  a 404. Now it exists
